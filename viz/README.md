# Interactive stories

Two versions of the same NSCH 2023–2025 story, built with D3.js v7, for comparison:

| Version | Page | Approach |
|---|---|---|
| **Charts** | `index.html` | Scrollytelling with the actual analyses: dot grids, adjusted gaps with confidence intervals, the model's odds ratios (forest plot) and a waterfall that builds each gap one factor at a time. |
| **The Room** | `people.html` | Every parent who answered, one circle each: 88,047 mothers and 43,550 fathers. Circles walk into groups by work and child's age as you scroll, and hovering over a circle shows that person's answers. Both the mothers' and fathers' sections end with a "Where would you stand?" picker. |

Both pages end with the same illustrated summary of what the survey can and cannot show (`snapshot.js`).

## Open it

Open either page in a browser with its full path, for example `file:///C:/Users/nyxro/early-parenting-research/viz/people.html`, or double-click the file. An internet connection is needed because D3 loads from the jsDelivr CDN.

## How The Room is built

- **Circles:** every circle is a real respondent from the public-use files: Adult 1, the child's biological or adoptive parent, with an employment status and a mental health rating.
- **Weighting:** circles are counted as people, but every percentage in the text, labels and picker uses the survey weights, as in the analysis. Groups with fewer than 30 respondents are not reported (NSCH reliability rule).
- **Layout:** each group is a disc laid out on a sunflower spiral, so every circle sits the same distance from its neighbors. One spacing applies to all groups in a view, chosen as the largest that fits. Ratings run from excellent in the center to poor at the rim, so the area of each color is proportional to the number of respondents.
- **Pizza:** the colored crowd is one disc cut into three slices, one per survey year (2023, 2024, 2025), each slice's angle in proportion to its respondents. Inside a slice, dots fill the arcs of a polar grid from the center out (one dot per spacing along each arc), so the ratings form rings from excellent in the middle to poor at the crust, and the rings line up across slices. A thin cut is kept clear between slices, and the year sits outside each slice. The spacing is the largest that fits, found by binary search like the other layouts.
- **Side by side:** in the mothers-and-fathers room, each work group gets a mothers column and a fathers column as a 100% stacked column of dots (excellent at the bottom, poor at the top). Every column has the same height, so each column has its own dot spacing; the label above it is the weighted share fair or poor.
- **Views:** a switcher on each stage (Crowd, Pizza, Years, Work, Child's age, Under 2; Fathers, Side by side) jumps to a view and stays in sync with scrolling. The Years view lines the circles up as a dot column chart by survey year and rating.
- **Rendering:** 130,000+ circles are drawn on one `<canvas>` per room (sharp on high-density screens). Each circle's start, end, size, color, delay and bend sit in typed arrays; each frame writes the circles straight into a pixel buffer (`ImageData`) and puts it on the canvas in one call. Circles whose cell is under 4 device pixels fill their whole cell and are antialiased by coverage (each pixel takes the share of it a circle covers), so dense discs, slices and columns read as even color without blotches or stripes; larger circles use a small cache of circle masks, and fathers stay square. Each room keeps its own copy of the positions, and the hover ring sits on its own canvas so hovering never redraws the circles. Colors are read from the CSS tokens and re-read when the theme changes. A quadtree finds the circle under the pointer, under the zoom.
- **Trickle:** between views every circle travels on its own slightly curved path (0.7 s). Its departure is delayed by its rank, so the new picture fills from the center (or the bottom) out, and the whole move takes about 3.5 s. One `requestAnimationFrame` loop computes all positions and draws them. A new step snaps the circles still in flight to where they were going, then starts the next move. With reduced motion (or Motion: off), circles jump straight to their places.
- **Text spacing:** the `TEXT` settings in `people.js` set the room around every label. The dots keep clear of the step cards, the zoom strip at the top and the legend at the bottom, measured on each step. Cluster label widths are estimated from their text, so layouts reserve room for them; narrow grids and phones put the count and share on two lines and row names above their rows. A last pass moves any label that still touches another down, and back inside the stage. Out-of-focus circles stay hoverable.
- **Zoom:** wheel with Ctrl/Cmd, pinch, or drag zooms and pans; the +, − and Fit buttons do the same, and double-clicking a group, slice or column zooms to it. Zoom is applied when the circles are drawn; labels are redrawn at the zoomed positions so text keeps its size. Every step change fits the view again. The stage takes keyboard focus: arrows move, + and − zoom, 0 or Escape fits, Enter zooms to each group in turn. On touch screens a tap opens a circle's tooltip, which stays until a tap elsewhere, a scroll or a new step.

## Design notes

- **Color:**
  - **Roles:** orange marks the highlighted outcome, or what widens a gap. Blue marks what narrows a gap or a lower odds ratio. Grays mark comparison groups.
  - **Validation:** colors come from the dataviz reference palette and were checked with its validator in light and dark mode.
- **Accessibility:**
  - Every chart has a legend and a data table.
  - Marks have hover and keyboard tooltips. The Room's circles are covered by its table instead of keyboard focus.
- **Mobile:** below 51.25rem wide, graphics pin to the top and text cards scroll below them. Long labels shorten or wrap.
