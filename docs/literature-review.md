# Literature for the Write-Up

**Status:** v0.1 (2026-11-11). DOIs checked against Crossref records (`api.crossref.org/works/<DOI>`), with PubMed or OpenAlex cross-checks where available. Summaries are based on retrieved abstracts unless noted. `[VERIFY]` marks details that could not be confirmed.

**CAUSAL DESIGN** marks quasi-experimental studies (difference-in-differences, synthetic control, instrumental variables, matching, natural experiments). Describe them as causal designs in their own settings. **Do not carry their causal language over to our associational NSCH results.**

Abbreviations: F1–F5 refer to our findings.
- **F1:** mothers looking for work have about twice the fair/poor rate of full-time mothers; the gap halves with adjustment.
- **F2:** mothers not looking for work differ from full-time mothers only when children are older.
- **F3:** part-time ≈ full-time.
- **F4:** full-time mothers of under-2s are not worse off.
- **F5:** fathers' non-employment gap is larger.

**Question wording** (from source 51):
- **Aggravation items:** "DURING THE PAST MONTH, how often have you felt…"
  - that this child is much harder to care for than most children their age? (`K8Q31`)
  - that this child does things that really bother you a lot? (`K8Q32`)
  - angry with this child? (`K8Q34`)
- **Coping item:** "How well do you think you are handling the day-to-day demands of raising children?" (`K8Q30`)
- **Child care item:** whether anyone in the family had to quit, not take, or greatly change a job because of problems with child care in the past 12 months (`K6Q27`). This is a paraphrase from the guide; check the questionnaire for exact wording `[VERIFY]`.

## Gaps

- **Newer state PFL programs:** no peer-reviewed evaluation of maternal mental health for New York, Washington or Massachusetts.
- **F2 (not looking, older children):** nothing addresses it directly. Thomas et al. (2005), Ross and Mirowsky (1995) and Frech and Damaske (2012) are the closest.
- **F5 in the U.S.:** U.S. evidence for a larger gap among fathers is thin. The one U.S. study found no gender difference (Aydiner-Avsar & Piovani, 2021).
- **Child care and maternal mental health:** little U.S. evidence beyond Herbst and Tekin (2014).
- **Origin and validity of the NSCH aggravation scale:** not documented in the sources found.
